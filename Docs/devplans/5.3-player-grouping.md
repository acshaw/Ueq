**Status:** ✅ Done 2026-07-26 — GP1–GP11 reviewed one at a time and confirmed (GP5 substantially redesigned

## Implementation log (2026-07-26)

Followed GP1–GP11 closely. Two refinements found necessary while writing the code, not anticipated at
review time:

- **GP5's threat-list ordering risk (flagged in the devplan's own grounding) was resolved more simply than
  sketched.** Rather than a separate `FinalThreatSnapshot` copy, `EnemyAI.OnDeath` just stops clearing
  `_threatList` at all (`AddThreat` already refuses once `_health.IsDead`, so nothing repopulates it) — a
  new `EnemyAI.ResolveCreditedGroup(NetworkIdentity killingBlowAttacker)` reads it directly and is called as
  a normal method from `MobKillReward.OnDeath`, so it's correct regardless of `IOnDeath` dispatch order
  between components on the mob, no snapshot copy needed.
- **GP11 (F1–F6) scope narrowed during implementation**: the devplan didn't anticipate that **players have
  no `Targetable` component** in this codebase (only mobs/NPCs do) — so routing F-key targeting through the
  existing click-to-target `Targetable`/`OnTargetChanged`/`TargetFrame` pipeline wasn't a drop-in fit, and
  `Targetable.SetHighlight`'s hardcoded red tint means "hostile," which would be actively confusing applied
  to a friendly group member. Implemented narrower: F-keys set `NetworkedPlayer.ServerTarget` directly
  (`CmdSetTarget` made `public`) for future systems (e.g. group-targetable abilities, GP9) to read, with a
  purely cosmetic local "selected slot" highlight in `PartyFrameUI` instead of touching `TargetFrame`. Live
  HP visibility for group members already comes from `PartyFrameUI`'s own frames (GP7), so no functionality
  was lost at the time. **Follow-up landed 2026-07-26, during 5.4's verification**: extending ability casting
  to target group members (needed for a Cleric to heal one) exposed that the narrower F-key-only approach
  wasn't enough on its own, so `Targetable` was added to players after all — friendly/green highlight vs. the
  hostile/red used for mobs, F-keys now route through the same `ApplyTarget`/`TargetFrame`/server-sync path a
  mouse click uses. See `docs/devplans/5.4-aggro-system.md`'s Verification log for the full writeup, including
  the necessary companion no-PvP friendly-fire broadening this required.

New: `PlayerParty.cs`, `PartyManager.cs` (`Assets/Scripts/Networking/`), `PartyFrameUI.cs`,
`PartyFrameSlotUI.cs` (`Assets/Scripts/UI/`, split into two files per the 5.10 HotbarUI/HotbarSlotUI lesson).
Modified: `ChatChannel.cs` (+`Group`), `ChatUI.cs` (filter/color/commands), `NetworkedPlayer.cs`
(`CmdSetTarget` public, `TryGetMobCorpse` credited-group check), `EnemyAI.cs` (`OnDeath` no longer clears
threat, `ResolveCreditedGroup`), `MobKillReward.cs` (full rewrite for GP4/GP5), `Corpse.cs`
(`SetEligibleLooters`/`CanLoot`, defaults to open-looting if never set — no regression for a mob with no
`MobKillReward`), `Health.cs` (friendly-fire backstop), `PlayerAutoAttack.cs` (proactive refusal message),
`GameNetworkManager.cs` (`PartyManager` lifecycle + disconnect-as-leave), `SceneSetup.cs` (`PlayerParty` on
the player prefab patch list + `CreatePlayer()`, `PartyManager` on the NetworkManager GameObject,
`CreatePartyFrameUI()` wired into `Tools/Build UI Scene`).

**NOT compiled/tested in-editor this session** — no Unity instance available. Compile-correct by careful
inspection (every new symbol cross-checked against its real definition, not guessed) and by re-reading each
modified region back after editing.

## Bug fix — solo players locked out of their own loot (2026-07-30, ✅ fixed + confirmed)

User reported Loot All (and per-slot Take) doing nothing — no items added, no error, loot window stuck open
forever — reproduced on the deployed server and a locally-hosted server with MPPM clients, always on a solo
kill. Root-caused via a temporary server-side deny-reason log added to `NetworkedPlayer`'s loot commands
(kept permanently, see below) rather than guessed: the log showed `CanLoot` returning `NotEligible` for the
player who had just delivered the killing blow.

**Mechanism:** `ResolveCreditedGroup` sums damage across *every* `_threatList` entry regardless of status
(5.4/AG4, intentional — a departed contributor should still get XP credit), and that list is only ever fully
cleared when a mob completes a full disengage-to-Idle cycle. A mob that stays engaged across multiple
separate, unrelated encounters (easy to hit during testing, plausible in real play) can carry a stale entry
whose tallied damage outweighs what the actual killer just dealt. The tie-break in `ResolveCreditedGroup`
only favors the killer on an *exact* damage tie, not "killer dealt less than some old, unrelated entry" — so
that stale entry "won" majority-damage credit and the real killer was excluded from `SetEligibleLooters`
entirely.

**Fix** (`MobKillReward.cs`): loot eligibility is now the majority-damage credited group **unioned with**
whoever/whichever party actually landed the killing blow — the killer (and their party) can always loot
their own kill, guaranteed, regardless of unrelated threat-list history. XP credit is untouched — it still
follows `ResolveCreditedGroup`'s result exactly as before, so legitimate multi-group contest fairness is
unaffected.

**Also hardened while diagnosing (kept permanently, independent of the above fix):** `TryGetMobCorpse`/
`TryGetPlayerCorpse` previously returned a bare `false` with zero player feedback for every denial reason
(corpse gone, not eligible, out of range) — this is what let the bug look like "stuck" instead of
diagnosable. They now report a specific `LootDenyReason`, every loot `[Command]`
(`CmdTakeLootSlot`/`CmdTakeLootCopper`/`CmdTakeLootAll`) messages the player with the specific reason, and
logs it server-side (`[Loot] ... denied looting ... — reason: ...`). `Corpse.TakeAll`/`PlayerCorpse.TakeAll`
now also return a blocked-item count so a partial Loot All (inventory full / an already-held LORE item) tells
the player instead of silently leaving items behind.

User confirmed fixed.

## Why

The near-term audience (per the 2026-07-26 ideation session) is the user + brother nightly, plus family
(including kids) on weekends — i.e. **multiple people playing together, not solo.** Grouping is the first
system that actually serves that directly: right now there's no way to share a kill, no group UI, and nothing
stops one family member's swing from landing on another. Sequenced ahead of real threat/aggro because it's
pure new systems/code (no existing placeholder to rip out), and ahead of 5.2 (level 1→2 polish) because that's
mostly hands-on feel-tuning rather than code Claude can drive efficiently.

Target scope (from the original Phase 2 design-doc line in `CLAUDE.md`, expanded during review): form/disband
a party (≤6), group member health frames in UI, shared XP + loot credit resolved by which group did the
majority of a mob's damage (covers contested/multi-group pulls), friendly-fire prevention, a full command set
(`/invite`/`/accept`/`/leave`/`/kick`/`/disband`/`/makeleader`/`/g`), and F1–F6 quick group-targeting.

## Current state (grounding — verified against the real code, not the design doc's assumptions)

- **XP on kill is single-attacker only.** `MobKillReward.OnDeath(NetworkIdentity attacker)` (`Assets/Scripts/
  Combat/MobKillReward.cs`) awards `def.xpReward` to exactly one `NetworkIdentity` — whoever's `Health.
  TakeDamage` call landed the killing blow. Faction hits are explicitly commented "killer-only for now, like
  XP" and stay that way (no clean case for splitting a standing change across a party).
- **`EnemyAI`'s `_threatList` (`Dictionary<NetworkIdentity, int>`) already tracks cumulative damage per
  attacker** — in this codebase, threat is currently a 1:1 mirror of raw damage dealt (the known placeholder
  the roadmap flags for the future real-threat rework). That makes it exactly the data GP5 needs for
  majority-damage group attribution, once grouped by party and read before it's cleared. It's currently
  private (no accessor) and gets cleared on death (`ClearAllThreat`, called from `EnemyAI.OnDeath`) — ordering
  against other `IOnDeath` subscribers on the same mob is not guaranteed, so a snapshot must be captured at
  the moment of death, not read lazily by another subscriber.
- **Mob corpse looting has NO ownership check today** — `NetworkedPlayer.TryGetMobCorpse` only checks range
  (`Vector3.Distance(...) > LootRange`) and `Corpse.IsActive`; any player in range can loot any mob corpse,
  first-come-first-served. (This is different from `PlayerCorpse`, a player's own death corpse, which *does*
  enforce `corpse.Owner != netIdentity`.) GP5 introduces the **first** ownership restriction on mob-corpse
  looting — not a generalization of an existing single-owner model, since there isn't one today.
- **`Health.TakeDamage(int amount, NetworkIdentity attacker = null)`** is the single shared damage-application
  path for every source (player auto-attack, mob auto-attack, ability effects, riposte counters). It has
  **zero PvP/friendly-fire awareness today** — nothing stops a player-owned `NetworkIdentity` from damaging
  another player. One choke point covers every current and future damage source for free.
- **No player roster/registry exists.** Server-side, the established idiom for "iterate connected players" is
  `NetworkServer.connections.Values` → `conn.identity.GetComponent<T>()` (used by `GameNetworkManager.
  SaveAllCharacters`, `ZoneManager.Update`). `ChatManager.FindConnectionByName(string)` is the existing
  "resolve a player by name" mechanism (backs `/whisper`) — the natural reuse point for `/invite <name>`.
  Client-side, there is no equivalent — party membership visible to a client must be explicitly synced down
  (SyncList, matching how `PlayerInventory`/`PlayerAbilities` already sync their own lists).
- **Chat commands are hand-rolled `if` blocks** in `ChatUI.TryHandleLocalCommand`, dispatching to a `Cmd*`
  method on whatever component owns the feature (`NetworkedPlayer.CmdUnstuck`/`CmdTravel`, or `PlayerSitting.
  CmdToggleSit` fetched via `LocalPlayer.Current.GetComponent<T>()`). New party commands follow this exact
  pattern and get appended to the `Commands` array that backs `/help`.
- **Chat delivery is already observation-independent** — `ChatManager.Deliver` sends via `conn.Send` (a raw
  `NetworkMessage`), not a `TargetRpc`/`ClientRpc`, specifically so it "survives SceneInterestManagement
  partitioning" (comment in `ChatManager.cs`, from the 3.0 zone-integration work). Confirmed still true this
  session. **This is why a group chat channel works across zones for free, while group health frames (SyncVar-
  based) don't** — the constraint is specific to SyncVar/SyncList/Rpc state on a `NetworkIdentity`, which only
  replicates to observers, not to chat.
- **`PlayerFrame`/`TargetFrame` bind via `LocalPlayer.Spawned`** (a static event exposing only *your own*
  `NetworkedPlayer`) then `Health.OnHealthUpdated` → an anchor-based fill bar (`Refresh(int current, int
  max)`). This idiom is directly reusable **per party member**, but `LocalPlayer` itself has no concept of
  "other players" — group frames need a new party-roster source, not a `LocalPlayer` extension.
- **`HotbarUI`'s key-polling pattern** (`Key[]` array indexed by slot, `Keyboard.current[key].
  wasPressedThisFrame` in `Update()`) is the established convention for slot-keyed input (also used by
  `PlayerAutoAttack`'s `digit1Key`, `PlayerSitting`'s `digit0Key`) — the model for GP11's F1–F6 targeting.
- **No persisted group state exists.** `CharacterSnapshot`/`CharacterRepository` (`Assets/Scripts/Server/
  Database/`) have no group/party field or child table — adding persistence would mean a new column/table,
  not just reusing something already there.
- **Zone/interest management constraint (real, not a preference — see GP7):** `ZoneInterestManagement :
  SceneInterestManagement` partitions observers by scene. A party member standing in a different zone is
  **not spawned/observed on your client at all** — Mirror won't even replicate their `NetworkIdentity` to
  you, so a naive "read `Health` off each member's `NetworkIdentity`" group frame silently breaks the moment
  members split zones.
- **`NetworkedPlayer.CurrentZoneId`** (a plain server-side property, not a SyncVar) plus the existing
  `EnemyAI.PruneZonedOutThreats` precedent (`ni.gameObject.scene != gameObject.scene`) give a ready-made
  "same zone" check for kill-range XP splitting.

## Decisions

### GP1 — Group lifecycle — **APPROVED: session-only**
Party dissolves on disconnect/logout; no DB schema change. Reversible later — persistence can be added as a
follow-on without touching the runtime party logic (it would just be a save/load of the same in-memory
shape).

### GP2 — Server data model — **APPROVED: `PartyManager` singleton + per-player `PlayerParty` SyncList**
A server-only `PartyManager` (mirrors the `ZoneManager`/`ChatManager` singleton convention) owns the
canonical `List<Party>` (`Party = { int Id, NetworkIdentity Leader, List<NetworkIdentity> Members }`, cap 6).
Each player gets a new `PlayerParty : NetworkBehaviour` component holding `readonly SyncList<NetworkIdentity>
_members` — Mirror has built-in `NetworkIdentity` read/write support, so this works the same way `SyncList
<InventorySlot>` already does. `PartyManager` pushes the current roster into every member's SyncList on any
change (join/leave/kick/disband/leader transfer) — gives clients a live, synced roster to build UI/targeting
off of.

### GP3 — Commands & permissions — **APPROVED: leader-gated, plus `/makeleader`**
- `/invite <name>` — **leader only** (solo player inviting someone becomes leader automatically). Resolves
  the target via `ChatManager.FindConnectionByName` (existing `/whisper` mechanism). Creates a pending invite
  (expires after ~60s or is replaced by a newer one) and messages the target: `"X invited you to a group.
  Type /accept to join."` — reuses the `/travel`-style "message via `SendSystemMsg`" pattern, no new UI.
- `/accept` — target joins if a pending invite exists and the party isn't full (6).
- `/leave` — any member leaves. If the leader leaves, promote the longest-standing remaining member. Solo
  remainder auto-disbands.
- `/disband` — leader only, dissolves the whole party.
- `/kick <name>` — leader only.
- `/makeleader <name>` — **leader only**, explicit leadership transfer to a named current member (in
  addition to the automatic promote-on-leave/disconnect fallback above).
- Disconnect is treated the same as `/leave` (hook into the existing `GameNetworkManager` disconnect handler).
- All commands get appended to `ChatUI`'s `Commands` array so `/help` stays accurate.

### GP4 — Shared XP split — **APPROVED: even split among same-zone credited-group members within 100 units, remainder to killer**
`MobKillReward.OnDeath` changes from "award `xpReward` to `attacker`" to: resolve the **credited group** (per
GP5, not simply `attacker`'s party), filter its members to those in the **same zone as the mob** (reusing the
`EnemyAI.PruneZonedOutThreats` scene-comparison idiom) **and** within **100 units** of the mob's death
position, then `AddXp(xpReward / eligibleCount)` to each, with the rounding remainder credited to the killer
(XP never evaporates to rounding). Faction hits stay killer-only, unchanged.

### GP5 — Multi-group contention: majority-damage group wins kill credit + loot rights — **APPROVED (redesigned during review)**
Surfaced during review: what happens when two separate groups (or an ungrouped solo player, treated as a
party of one) both engage the same mob? Resolution:
- On death, group `EnemyAI`'s threat-list entries (already = cumulative damage per attacker, see grounding)
  by party — an ungrouped attacker counts as its own party of one, per the user's framing.
- Sum damage per group; the group with the **highest total** is the **credited group** — it receives GP4's
  XP split (among its own same-zone/in-range members) **and exclusive looting rights** on the corpse.
- **Tie-break (exact equal damage, rare):** the killing-blow attacker's group wins — reuses the single
  `attacker` identity already passed to `OnDeath`, no extra bookkeeping.
- **Implementation note:** `EnemyAI` must capture a snapshot of the threat list at the moment of death
  (before `ClearAllThreat` runs, and independent of `IOnDeath` subscriber ordering) and expose it (e.g. a
  public `FinalThreatSnapshot` populated right as death is detected) for `MobKillReward`/`Corpse` to read.
- **Loot rights**: `Corpse` gains a snapshotted set of eligible looter `NetworkIdentity`s (the credited
  group's members, captured at death — not a live party reference, so a later party change doesn't
  retroactively add/remove looting rights on an already-dead corpse). `TryGetMobCorpse` in `NetworkedPlayer`
  gains a membership check alongside its existing range check — this is a **new** restriction (see grounding:
  mob corpses currently have none).

### GP6 — Friendly-fire prevention — **APPROVED: two-layer guard**
1. **Hard backstop in `Health.TakeDamage`**: if `attacker` and the damaged object are both players and share
   a party, no-op before mutating `_current` — a single choke point that covers every current and future
   damage source (auto-attack, ability effects, riposte counters) for free.
2. **UX-friendly refusal at the call site**: `PlayerAutoAttack.Update()`'s existing target-validation block
   (which already messages the player for out-of-range/no-LOS) gets a same-party check too, so a player
   attacking a group member sees a clear chat message instead of silently whiffing every swing forever.
Ability-effect targeting (`DamageEffect` via `PlayerAbilities.TryCast`) relies on layer 1's backstop only for
now — it has no friend/foe validation at all today, and proactive messaging there is a small, separable
follow-up if it turns out to matter in practice.

### GP7 — Group member health frames vs. zone-partitioned observation — **APPROVED: same-zone-only, greyed placeholder elsewhere**
Because `ZoneInterestManagement` partitions Mirror's observers by scene, a party member in a different zone
is never replicated to your client — their `Health`/`Nameplate` simply don't exist on your side. Group frames
show live HP/name for members in your current zone; members elsewhere show a lightweight greyed
name-only placeholder — the same approach classic EverQuest used. Zero new networking — just a client-side
"is this `NetworkIdentity` currently spawned/observed" check (`NetworkClient.spawned.ContainsKey`) per member.
Not a one-way door — live cross-zone data (a dedicated `conn.Send`-style push, mirroring how chat already
works) can be added later without touching the party data model, if it turns out to matter.

Confirmed this constraint is specific to SyncVar/SyncList/Rpc-based state, not chat — see the grounding note
above. Worth remembering as a standing rule for any future feature that reads another player's `NetworkIdentity`
state directly.

New `PartyFrameUI` — a small vertical stack of up to 5 member frames (self stays in the existing `PlayerFrame`
top-left, and is also always F1 / slot 1, see GP11). Each frame reads its target member's `NetworkIdentity`
from the local `PlayerParty` SyncList, resolves `GetComponent<Health>()`/`GetComponent<Nameplate>()` if
spawned/observed, and reuses the exact `Refresh(int current, int max)` anchor-fill idiom from `PlayerFrame`/
`TargetFrame` — no new bar-rendering code, just a new binding source.

### GP8 — Invite/roster feedback stays chat-only — **APPROVED**
No dedicated invite-popup UI for v1. Consistent with the game's existing chat-driven philosophy. **Flagged
for later**: once the group system is fleshed out, a dedicated group-management UI pane is likely (buttons
for invite/kick/leave/etc., wired to the same underlying commands built here) — deliberately not built now,
to keep this pass trim; see Deferred/backlog seeds.

### GP9 — Out of scope for this pass (narrowed during review) — **DECIDED**
No auto-summon/teleport-to-leader, no extending ability targeting (e.g. Cleric's Minor Heal is still
self-only per 3.1.5) to group members — both are natural fast-follows **unblocked by** this item, not part of
it.

### GP10 — Group chat channel — **APPROVED (pulled into scope during review)**
New `ChatChannel.Group` value (alongside the existing Say/Shout/Whisper/System/Combat/Reward/Ability/
Environment/NPC set in `ChatChannel.cs`). Unlike `/say`/`/shout` (range-based), delivery must reach every
party member **regardless of zone** — the whole point of a group channel is staying in touch across a split
party — so it reuses the same **observation-independent `conn.Send` delivery** `ChatManager` already uses for
whispers/system messages. New `/g <message>` command resolves the sender's party via `PartyManager`/
`PlayerParty` and delivers to each member's connection directly.

### GP11 — F1–F6 quick group-targeting — **APPROVED (pulled into scope during review)**
Mirrors the existing hotbar key-polling pattern (`HotbarUI.cs`: a `Key[]` array indexed by slot, polled via
`Keyboard.current[key].wasPressedThisFrame` in `Update()`) rather than inventing a new input mechanism — a
`GroupTargetKeys = { Key.F1, ..., Key.F6 }` array, polled client-side. **F1 always targets self**, and self
always occupies slot 1 **from that client's own point of view** — i.e. each client locally reorders the
synced `PlayerParty` roster (self first, other members filling slots 2–6 in a stable order, e.g. by netId) so
"group member 1" means "me" identically for everyone, not a shared/global slot number. Pressing a key resolves
the corresponding `NetworkIdentity` from that local ordering and calls the existing `CmdSetTarget` — no new
server-side targeting logic, just a new client-side input→target mapping. This only sets your target; it does
not by itself let you cast heals/buffs on a group member (that's GP9's deferred ability-targeting extension)
— useful now regardless, for inspecting/following a member and for when that extension lands.

## Build / test plan
1. **Data model**: `PartyManager` singleton + `PlayerParty` component (added to the player prefab patch list,
   same convention as every other `NetworkBehaviour` addition in this project).
2. **Commands**: `/invite`, `/accept`, `/leave`, `/disband`, `/kick`, `/makeleader`, `/g` wired through
   `ChatUI` → `NetworkedPlayer`/`PlayerParty` `Cmd*` methods, `/help` table updated.
3. **Multi-group kill attribution**: `EnemyAI` threat-snapshot-on-death + party grouping + majority-damage
   resolution (GP5); verify a solo kill is unaffected, and a two-group contest (e.g. two separate MPPM
   clients/parties both damaging one mob) correctly credits the higher-damage group, with a forced-tie case
   falling back to the killing blow's group.
4. **XP split**: `MobKillReward.OnDeath` reworked per GP4; verify a grouped kill splits evenly among the
   credited group's same-zone/in-range members, with the killer absorbing the remainder.
5. **Loot rights**: `Corpse` + `TryGetMobCorpse` gain the credited-group membership check; verify a
   non-credited player (including a losing second group) cannot loot the corpse, and a party change after
   death doesn't retroactively change who can loot it.
6. **Friendly fire**: `Health.TakeDamage` backstop + `PlayerAutoAttack` proactive message; verify group
   members can't damage each other, non-group players still can.
7. **UI**: `PartyFrameUI` stack; verify frames appear/disappear on join/leave/kick/disband/leadership
   transfer, show live HP for same-zone members, and a greyed placeholder for out-of-zone members.
8. **F1–F6 targeting**: verify F1 always selects self on every client regardless of join order, F2–F6 select
   other members consistently, and the existing target frame/UI updates correctly off an F-key press.
9. **Group chat**: verify `/g` reaches all party members regardless of zone.
10. **Multi-client test** (MPPM, per this project's established pattern): form a 3-person party across 2+
    zones, plus a second contending group/solo player on a shared mob, and confirm every behavior above end
    to end — not just a compile check.

## Done when
A party of up to 6 can form via `/invite`+`/accept` and be managed via `/kick`/`/leave`/`/disband`/
`/makeleader`; shows live member health frames (same-zone) or placeholders (other-zone) plus F1–F6 quick
targeting; a mob kill is credited (XP split + exclusive loot rights) to whichever group dealt the majority of
its damage, correctly handling solo players as parties-of-one and ties via killing blow; friendly-fire damage
is blocked in both directions; and `/g` reaches the whole party regardless of zone. Verified via a multi-
client (MPPM) round-trip, including a genuine two-group contested-kill scenario, not just a compile check.
