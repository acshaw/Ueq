# 8.3 — Level variance within one mob type at a spawn

**Status:** 🟡 Awaiting review — scope grew 2026-09-20 (see LV2a).
**Roadmap item:** M8 → 8.3
**Pattern:** a per-instance override on the spawned `GameObject`, never a mutation of the shared
`MobDefinition` — that object is a single runtime instance reused by every concurrent spawn of that mob
id (`MobRegistry.Build` runs once per DB row, not once per spawn), so writing to it directly would leak
one instance's roll into every other instance of the same mob type currently alive anywhere in the world.

**Scope grew 2026-09-20:** asked the user how the level roll should actually be visible to players (the
devplan's own flagged risk — no signal without *something*). Answer: extend `/con` with EQ1-style
level-based color/text (LV2a), not a bare nameplate number. That's a real addition on top of the original
spawn-time roll — touches `PlayerConsider.cs` and `CombatResolver.cs`, not just `SpawnPoint`/`SpawnTable` —
flagged explicitly below rather than folded in without comment.

---

## Goal & why now

Today a mob type has exactly one authored level (`MobDefinition.mobLevel`), so every wolf at a "wolf camp"
is the identical level. Real camps often want organic variance (a pack that's level 4-6, not five copies
of level 5) both for feel and because it's a real gameplay lever (a level-differential-driven hit-roll
adjustment, see below) — this item adds a per-spawn-instance level roll, scoped deliberately narrow.

## Current state (grounding) — the fork this item hinges on

Traced exactly what `mobLevel` drives before designing anything, since the answer changes the scope a lot:

- `CombatResolver.BuildCombatant` reads `def.mobLevel` into `Combatant.Level`, which feeds
  `ApplyLevelDifferential` (the Fibonacci-band hit-roll adjustment based on the level **gap** between
  attacker and defender, per the 5.1.1 Hit Roll devplan). **So level is a real combat-math input.**
- **But** `Combatant.Atk`/`Dodge`/`Parry`/`Riposte`/`Ac` are all read from `def.atk`/`def.avoidanceDodge`/
  etc. **directly** — authored flat numbers, not derived from `mobLevel` via any formula (this was a
  deliberate, twice-repeated design choice: AD3 for Atk, AV3 for avoidance, both reasoned as "mobs have
  nothing to derive them from"). Likewise `maxHealth`/`attackDamage`/`xpReward` are flat authored fields,
  not level-formula outputs.
- **Conclusion:** varying `mobLevel` per spawned instance changes that instance's level-differential
  hit-roll term (a real mechanical effect — a level-6 wolf hits/dodges relatively better against a given
  player than a level-4 one of the *same* authored Atk/Dodge, purely via the differential) and its
  nameplate/con-color display, but does **not** automatically scale its HP/damage/loot — those stay
  whatever the single `MobDefinition` says, identical across every leveled instance, unless this item
  *also* builds mob stat-scaling-by-level (a formula system for mobs that has been deliberately avoided
  twice already).

## Decisions (recommendations in **bold**)

**LV1 — Scope: level display + hit-roll differential only, NOT stat scaling.** **Recommend shipping the
narrow version** — a per-instance level override that feeds `Combatant.Level` and the nameplate, leaving
HP/Atk/AC/loot exactly as authored. Consistent with the AD3/AV3 precedent (mobs are flat authored data,
not formula outputs) and ships something real and useful (organic level range, a genuine differential
effect) without reopening "should mobs have HP-per-level formulas" — a much bigger, unrequested rework.
**If you actually want the wolves to also hit harder/tankier at the high end of their range, say so** — that's
a legitimately bigger item (a mob-stat-scaling formula, its own devplan) and shouldn't be bundled into this
one silently.

**LV2 — Where the override lives.** **A new field on `MobApplicator`** (`int? LevelOverride`), not on
`MobDefinition` (shared, would leak across instances) and not a new standalone component (`MobApplicator`
already is the per-instance definition-holder every mob has — `EnemyAI`/`Health`/`NpcFaction` already
resolve through it, so this is the natural home). `CombatResolver.BuildCombatant` reads
`mobApp.LevelOverride ?? def.mobLevel`.

**LV2a — Visible signal: extend `/con` (the existing consider mechanic) with level, EQ1-style — not a
nameplate readout.** Asked the user how a spawned mob's rolled level should actually be visible to
players; answer was to extend the existing right-click/`C`-key consider mechanic (`PlayerConsider.cs`,
5.4/AG1) so level — not just faction standing — colors and words the message, the classic EQ1 "con"
pattern. This is a materially bigger addition than a bare `Nameplate` label would have been, so scoping it
explicitly rather than folding it in silently:

- **Grounded first, not invented from scratch:** `CombatResolver.cs` already has a level-gap-to-severity
  concept — `IncrementsToFutility`/the `fraction` computed inside `ApplyLevelDifferential` (5.1.1, HR3) is
  exactly "how significant is this level gap," already tuned via the Fibonacci band floors/increments and
  already asymmetric (disadvantage hurts faster than advantage helps, `LevelAdvantageScale`). **Recommend
  extracting that into a new public `CombatResolver.LevelGapFraction(levelA, levelB)`** (0 = even, 1 = full
  band futility), with `ApplyLevelDifferential` refactored to call it instead of duplicating the
  computation — so `/con`'s color/text is **mechanically consistent with what actually happens in combat**,
  not a separately-invented scale that could drift from the real hit-roll effect.
- **Band scheme — 6 tiers, asymmetric by design (the user's explicit ask):** the favors-you side gets three
  gradations (a genuine "risky but winnable" tier between even and comfortable), the favors-mob side stays
  two — not mirrored/symmetric unless asked for later. Direction from `gap = playerLevel - mobLevel`
  (matches `ApplyLevelDifferential`'s own attacker/defender convention), severity from `LevelGapFraction`:

  | gap | fraction | Color | Hex | Text |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | < 0 | ≥ 0.5 | Red | `#FF4D4D` | "It would be a great danger to you." |
  | < 0 | < 0.5 | Yellow | `#FFD24D` | "It could be dangerous." |
  | 0 | — | White | `#FFFFFF` | "It looks like an even match for you." |
  | > 0 | < 0.25 | Light Blue | `#99D6FF` | "It looks like a difficult fight, but you should prevail." |
  | > 0 | 0.25–<0.5 | Blue | `#4DA6FF` | "It shouldn't be much of a challenge for you." |
  | > 0 | ≥ 0.5 | Green | `#33CC33` | "It would be a trivial kill for you." |

  No "gray/trivial" band (below Green) — this game's XP model has no level-vs-mob XP-cutoff concept to
  hang that on, unlike EQ1's; noting the omission rather than inventing one.

- **Stitching, not concatenation — the user caught this before implementation.** The faction sentence
  (`FactionThreshold.ConsiderText`, e.g. "regards you indifferently", composed today as
  `"{label} {considerText}."`) and the level sentence must read as one natural message regardless of which
  of the 9 faction phrases × 6 level bands pairs up. Repeating `{label}` in both clauses reads redundant
  ("Giant Rat regards you indifferently. Giant Rat would be a trivial kill for you."), so **the level
  sentence is a pronoun continuation ("It …") appended to the same message**, not a repeated subject and
  not a second chat line — e.g. *"Giant Rat regards you indifferently. It would be a trivial kill for
  you."* Only the second clause gets the level-band color; the faction clause keeps the channel's existing
  plain color.
- **Implementation shape — no `ChatMessage`/`ChatUI.cs` changes needed.** Checked `ChatMessage.cs` (no
  per-message color field, only `Channel`/`SenderName`/`Text`) and `ChatUI.cs`'s `Consider` render arm
  (`$"<color=#{hex}>{msg.Text}</color>"`, one color for the whole channel). Rather than adding a
  per-message color field and threading it through the render switch, **build the level-band `<color>` tag
  directly into `msg.Text`** server-side in `CmdConsider` — TMP nests rich-text `<color>` tags correctly, so
  an inner tag inside the channel's existing outer wrap just works, zero rendering-pipeline changes.
  `CmdConsider` reads the caller's own level (`GetComponent<PlayerExperience>()?.Level`) and the target's
  resolved level (`mobApp.LevelOverride ?? mobApp.Definition?.mobLevel`), appends `" It {phrase}"` after the
  existing faction sentence, inside the same message/same chat line.
- **Player-vs-player /con** (a faction-bearing NPC is required today — `CmdConsider` early-returns if
  `NpcFaction` is missing) stays unaffected; the level sentence only applies when considering an
  `NpcFaction`-bearing target, same gate the mechanic already has.

**LV3 — Authoring shape.** **`MinLevelOverride`/`MaxLevelOverride` on `SpawnTableEntry`** (nullable ints;
both null = no variance, spawns at the mob's authored level exactly, today's behavior unchanged). At
`SpawnOne` time, if set, roll `Random.Range(min, max+1)` and call `mobApp.SetLevelOverride(roll)` right
after `SetDefinition`, before `NetworkServer.Spawn` — same "configure-before-Spawn" seam
`ApplyPatrol`/`ApplyWanderRegion` already use. The `mobId`-only (non-table) spawn path has no entry to
source a range from — stays unaffected, exactly today's fixed-level behavior.

**LV4 — Validation.** **Clamp `max >= min` in the API's `Normalize`/`Update`** (same pattern as the
existing `Blank`/standing-default clamps in `MobsController`), and treat `0`/unset as "no override" rather
than a literal level-0 mob — mirrors how `weight`/`groupSize` already default sanely.

## Schema

```sql
ALTER TABLE spawn_table_entries
    ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS min_level_override INT,   -- null = no variance (spawn at mob's authored level)
    ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS max_level_override INT;
```

## Files (once approved)

**Modified:** `MobApplicator.cs` (`LevelOverride`/`SetLevelOverride`), `CombatResolver.cs`
(`BuildCombatant`'s mob branch reads the override; new public `LevelGapFraction`, `ApplyLevelDifferential`
refactored to call it), `SpawnTable.cs` (`SpawnTableEntry` gains the two level-range fields),
`SpawnTableRepository.cs`/`SpawnTableRegistry.cs` (read them), `SpawnPoint.cs` (`SpawnOne` rolls +
applies), `PlayerConsider.cs` (`CmdConsider` appends the level-banded sentence — LV2a), migration
`00NN_spawn_level_variance.sql`, API entity/controller (+ min≤max validation) + Angular service/editor (two
new number inputs per entry row, "Level override min/max — blank = mob's own level").

## Test plan

1. Author an entry with `MinLevelOverride=4, MaxLevelOverride=6` on an otherwise-normal mob. Spawn a group
   (groupSize > 1) → `/con` (or right-click) each member → confirm different members show different
   level-banded colors/text, matching their actual rolled level relative to the player's own.
2. Confirm HP/damage/loot are identical across the level-4 and level-6 rolls (LV1 — no accidental scaling
   crept in) — only the hit-roll differential and the `/con` message differ.
3. An entry with both level fields null/unset behaves exactly as today (regression check) — spawns at the
   mob's authored `mobLevel`, `/con` shows whichever band that authored level lands in (unchanged from
   what LV2a adds to *every* NPC consider, not just leveled spawns).
4. `/con` on a target at each of the 6 bands (even, a small/large gap favoring the player, a small/large
   gap favoring the mob, and specifically the new light-blue "slightly favors you" band at a small
   fraction) — confirm the color/text and the stitched sentence (`"{faction sentence} It {level phrase}"`)
   read naturally in every combination, and that the direction (blue/green vs. yellow/red) matches who's
   actually favored in a real fight (cross-check against the Combat Simulator if easier than fighting it
   out).
5. Consider a non-faction target (if any exist) — confirm no crash, same early-return as today.

## Risks

- **Scope creep back into stat scaling** — LV1's boundary is deliberate; if implementation starts touching
  `maxHealth`/`atk`/`ac`, that's a scope change worth flagging back to the user, not sliding into quietly.
- **LV2a's exact band thresholds/text are a proposal, not yet confirmed** — reasonable defaults grounded in
  the existing `LevelGapFraction`/Fibonacci machinery, but the specific 0.5 cutoff and wording are new
  content this devplan is introducing, not something already decided elsewhere. Easy to retune later (one
  small table), not worth blocking on.
- **`LevelGapFraction`'s refactor touches `ApplyLevelDifferential`, live combat-math code** — low risk (it's
  an extraction, not a behavior change — same formula, just named and exposed), but worth the test plan's
  step 4 cross-check against the Combat Simulator to confirm the refactor didn't subtly change hit-roll
  behavior for existing combat.

## Done when

A spawn table entry can specify a level range; spawned instances roll independently within it, affecting
their hit-roll level-differential and `/con`'s color/text (EQ1-style, mechanically consistent with the same
Fibonacci-band math that already drives the hit-roll adjustment), with zero change to HP/damage/AC/loot and
zero change to entries that don't opt in.
